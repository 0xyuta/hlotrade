# HLO — fee counting and rewards files for many wallets

Asked for by the owner on 2026-10-31 ("make it large scale for many users, ready for mainnet"). No contract changes. The checkpoint rules do not change: one signed checkpoint per minute, a minute's fees are its counted API fills, checkpoints are never revised.

## 1. The problem and the target

Today every registered wallet costs one `userFillsByTime` call a minute. Hyperliquid allows 1,200 weight a minute per IP and the call weighs at least 20, so about 50 registered wallets stall the poster (`docs/OPS.md` §2, "Scale limit" then, "Scale" now). Other parts also grow with every registered wallet: the verifier's hourly omission pass, `ingest.json` (rewritten after each wallet), the registry listing (one R2 list page per 1,000 wallets, every minute), and `latest.json` (a full Merkle dump and every user's entry; every browser downloads it, about 40 MB at 100,000 users).

**Target:** 100,000 registered wallets on one poster server and one verifier server, at the same cost per checkpoint as about 50 today when few of them trade.

**Capacity after this work (one server, 50 fill reads a minute):**

| Distinct wallets trading | Result |
|---|---|
| up to about 50 a minute, sustained | checkpoints stay on time |
| more than that | rewards run late by about (distinct wallets active in that period) ÷ 50 minutes, then catch up; nothing is lost. 3,000 distinct traders in an hour means rewards about an hour late |
| far beyond that | our own Hyperliquid node (§9), a later decision with a monthly cost |

## 2. What we will not do: more IP addresses

Hyperliquid's Terms (last updated June 15, 2026) §3.1.8 forbid "bots, scripts, or other automated methods" that "bypass rate limits". Spreading fill reads over a pool of IP addresses to get more than one IP's allowance is that. HLO follows Hyperliquid's terms, so there is no IP pool, and the note in `docs/OPS.md` suggesting one goes. The poster and the verifier stay separate services on separate servers because they are independent checks of each other (`docs/OPS.md`), each within its own IP's limit.

## 3. Facts this relies on (measured 2026-10-31)

- **Public trade feed.** The WebSocket `trades` subscription needs one `coin` per subscription and each trade carries `users: [buyer, seller]`. On mainnet, 179 live perps on one connection for 45 s: 136 trades/s, 37 KB/s, every trade with `users`. Limits per IP: 1,000 subscriptions, 10 connections.
- **Builder fee total.** `referral(builder).builderRewards` is cumulative and exact: on testnet 86.26939, which equals `claimed + unclaimed`, and equals to the last digit the summed `builderFee` of the two wallets that traded through HLO (35.19181 + 51.07758). A fill does not name its builder: a third wallet's 0.528985 of builder fees went to other builders and is not in the total.
- UNCONFIRMED: how quickly `builderRewards` moves after a fill, and that it behaves the same on mainnet. This spec only uses it for an hourly alert, with a tolerance (§6).

## 4. The trade feed (`ops/src/tradefeed.ts`)

A class used by the poster and the verifier, each with its own connection.

- Subscribes `trades` for every core perp in `meta` that is not delisted.
- **`meta` and new listings.** `meta` is read about once a minute (checked on the ping tick) and also on demand (see Delivered through). A coin found by a read is subscribed, and counts as a gap from the **start** of the previous successful read to its acknowledgment (a read's time is when its request began): a new listing's first minutes are never silently unwatched. The same holds when a reconnect's read finds a coin the lost connection never subscribed to: that connection is trusted only up to the previous read. When no read has succeeded for 3 minutes, no period, closed or open, counts as watched past the last good read (less the 5 s margin): the poster then takes its wider reads, so a `meta` outage cannot stall it, whether the socket is up, closed or gapped. Nothing is stored for this, so a good read that shows nothing new restores it.
- `seen: Map<user, lastTradeMs>` for both sides of every trade, from any wallet (a wallet may register just after its first trade). `usersSince(ms)` returns the users whose last trade is at or after `ms`: a superset of who traded in a window, which is safe. `prune(ms)` drops older entries.
- Pings every 15 s; no pong within 30 s closes the socket. Reconnects with a 1, 2, 4 … 30 s backoff, which starts over only once the feed is live. **Watchdog:** a socket that is not live 30 s after it was created, or after a new coin was subscribed, is closed and reconnected (a refused or unanswered subscription, or a socket that never opens, would otherwise keep the feed down for as long as pongs keep the socket up); a refused coin is logged.
- **Coverage.** A connection is live once every subscription has been acknowledged. A live period is watched from 5 s after it began until 5 s before it ended (a period of 10 s or less is not kept: it has no coverage left); these are wall-clock health measurements, the one exception to "now is chain time", and the 5 s margin covers the difference. The open period has no end margin yet. `uncovered(fromMs, toMs)` returns how many ms of the window were not watched; before the first live moment everything is uncovered. A period ends at the last time the socket is known to have been alive, which is **the last message of any kind it delivered**, never at the moment a loss was noticed: this holds for every way a connection is lost (a close or error event, a missing pong noticed up to 30 s late, a stop). A socket that has delivered nothing for 20 s is not counted as watching (the open period ends at its last message, before any close; this also covers the machine sleeping), and if it then delivers again the quiet stretch stays a gap. A healthy socket delivers a pong every 15 s, so it never trips this.
- **Silence** (`TradeFeedDeps.maxSilenceMs`, optional, default none). Pongs prove the socket is up, not that its subscriptions still deliver. If Hyperliquid silently dropped the `trades` subscriptions while pongs kept coming, the socket would stay live with gap 0: the poster would read only `usersSince` (nobody) and publish empty minutes, the verifier's omission pass would be blind the same way (its list is `usersSince` plus the tables), and a published checkpoint is never revised, so those fees would be lost for good. So on the ping tick, a live socket whose newer of (a readable trade last arrived on it, it went live) is at least `maxSilenceMs` ago is logged (`trade feed: no trades for <N> s on a live socket, reconnecting`) and dropped and reconnected like any other loss. Its live period ends **when that last readable trade arrived**, not at the last pong: those seconds were not watched, so `uncovered` over the silent stretch is not 0, from 5 s before that trade onwards. `deliveredPast` is unchanged: a closed socket's last message, a pong included, still counts. A trade entry that cannot be read does not count as a trade; a socket that is not live is the watchdog's, not this rule's; and the clock starts afresh on every new socket, so a feed that stays silent reconnects about once per interval (30 to 45 s live, a second of backoff) and never in a faster loop, and its runs of gap minutes then reach the poster's long branch (§5). The tick is every 15 s, so detection takes 30 to 45 s; a minute that is published before the drop is noticed was read from a list that lacks the wallets whose trades were not seen, so the rule bounds the loss to one minute per event, up to about 35 s of trades by wallets not otherwise read; it does not remove it. A real halt of the exchange looks the same: after about 7 minutes it reaches the long branch though nobody traded, which is the fail-safe side (about 33 to 66 hours late at 100,000 wallets). On 999 `maxSilenceMs` is 30 s (mainnet's rate was measured at 136 trades a second across 179 perps; a read-only run of 190 s on 2026-10-31, 07:36 UTC, a quiet Saturday hour at 21 trades a second, saw a longest silence of 0.8 s and no second without a trade, so 30 s is far from a normal gap). On 998 it is off: testnet has quiet minutes.
- **Trades it cannot read.** A `trades` entry that is not a readable trade (not an object, `time` not a finite number, `users` not two strings), or a `data` that is not a list, is logged once per connection (`trade feed: unreadable trade`, at most 200 characters) and is a gap: the live period ends now and a new one starts at once, so the margins make at least 10 s uncovered. Without this a changed field name would drop every trade while pongs keep the feed looking healthy. Malformed JSON and unknown channels are ignored.
- **Delivered through.** `deliveredPast(ms)` is true when (a) a message of any kind (a pong, a trade, an acknowledgment) was heard at or after `ms` + 5 s (wall clock), on any socket, or a trade timed more than 5 s after `ms` has arrived: nothing older is still on the way, and a closed socket's last message still counts, so a window covered through it never waits for a message that cannot come; and (b) a `meta` read that **started after `ms`** has succeeded, so a coin listed before `ms` was found and its cut made before the call returns true. When only (b) is missing, the call starts a read if none is in flight (and not within 5 s of a failed one). The owner asks `uncovered` again after `deliveredPast` turns true. (A period ends 5 s before its last message, so a window covered through that message is delivered too.) A trade timed more than 5 s ahead of the clock does not count for (a), so one bad time cannot make every minute delivered. The owner marks a minute with `setCovered(end)` at the moment it makes its list, before it reads anything. From then on a trade timed at or before `end` is late: it is on no list, it counts in `lateTrades`, and `takeLate()` gives its two wallets (lowercase, once, then the set is empty). The owner reads those wallets too before it marks the minute covered, and alerts when `lateTrades` grew.
- Nothing is stored on disk: a restart is a gap.
- Config: `TRADE_FEED` `on` or `off`. Default `on` on 998 and 999, `off` on anvil (the e2e's fake Hyperliquid has no socket); refused `off` on 999. The URL comes from `HL_API` (`https` → `wss`, plus `/ws`).

If the feed is down for more than 5 minutes the system falls back to reading every wallet, which is today's behaviour: it fails safe, only slower (§5).

## 5. The poster

**Which wallets a minute reads.** For minute M ending at `end` (ms), with `coveredThrough` the end of the last minute that was fully handled (in `ingest.json`):

The minute's **gap** is `uncovered(coveredThrough, end)`: how much of its window (the time since the last covered minute, one minute when the poster is in step) the feed was not watching. A **run** is a stretch of consecutive minutes that each have a gap; a fully watched minute (gap 0) ends it.

- No gap: the wallets in `usersSince(coveredThrough)` that are registered. The poster waits (as it does for the grace) until `deliveredPast(end)`.
- A gap, with at most 5 minutes unwatched in the run: those wallets, plus every wallet that traded or registered in the last 24 hours (`recent`, kept in `ingest.json`).
- More than 5 minutes unwatched in the run, or `TRADE_FEED=off`, or nothing covered yet: every registered wallet. Only the first case alerts (`TRADE_FEED=off` and a fresh or migrated state read every wallet silently), with one alert an hour whose text carries no number ("the trade feed has not been watching for over 5 minutes").

**Why a run.** One window is one minute, so a single window can never show more than 60 s unwatched however long the feed has been down; measured on it, the 5 minutes would never be reached and an outage would be read like a short gap for ever, losing the fees of every wallet that was idle for a day and started trading during it. The run adds the gaps of the minutes already marked covered (in memory, as `gapRunMs`) to this minute's and to those of the finished minutes after it, up to the first fully watched one: a poster that was down for an hour reads everyone on its first minute, not after publishing minutes that a wider read can no longer fix. A minute counts once, when its wallets have been read and it is marked covered: a read that fails and is retried does not count its gap again, and a retry after a failure that came later (the minute is already covered) does not end the run. A minute that reads every wallet does not end the run: while the outage lasts every minute reads every wallet (a wallet already read past the minute costs no call), and only a fully watched minute ends it. The run is not saved. A restart's new feed has everything before it unwatched, so downtime is found by the look-ahead; a restart in the middle of an outage starts counting again. The run starts at `coveredThrough` (up to about 70 s before the stop) and ends at the new feed's first live moment plus its margin, so the downtime that stays on the short path is about 3 minutes (3.5 to 4.5 in practice), not 5.

Each wallet read is fetched through the newest finished minute, as today, so a wallet costs one call however many minutes it covers. When a poster is behind, the next minutes need no more calls and it publishes in a burst.

Then the wallets of any trade of the minute that the feed heard while the reads were running (`takeLate`, asked again until it gives none, with no wait between the last ask and the next step) are read too. After all of a minute's wallets are read: `ingest.markCovered(end)`, `feed.prune(coveredThrough − 1 h)`. A minute whose wallets an earlier tick already read (that tick failed after marking it) reads nothing and waits for nothing, so a dead feed cannot hold it. The `checkpoint M:` log line says how many wallets were actually fetched, so a minute published in a burst says `read 0`.

**Pacing.** Fill reads go through a pacer at `HL_FILLS_PER_MINUTE` (default 50, which leaves room for other requests); a response of more than 20 fills adds its extra weight (1 per 20 items) to the wait. The 429 retry stays as the safety net.

**`ingest.json` version 2.** `{v: 2, coveredThrough, cursors, recent, rows}`. `fetchedThrough(user) = max(cursors[user], coveredThrough)`; `markCovered` drops cursors at or below `coveredThrough`, so only wallets ahead of it are stored. A version 1 file is read with `coveredThrough = −1` and its cursors kept: no refetch. Saved at most every 10 s and at the end of a pass (rows and cursors are saved together, so a crash refetches and never counts twice), not after every wallet.

**Late fills.** Unchanged from today: a fill the feed or the API shows after its minute was published is not counted; the verifier alerts. This is the limit the minute-settlement design already lists; its likely new cause is a very short feed gap with a wallet idle for over 24 hours.

## 6. The verifier

- **Row checks with one read per wallet.** A row is checked against fills read through the newest finished minute and cached per wallet, so several minutes of one wallet cost one call. The cache drops what is older than the minute being checked. **A shortfall read from the cache (table above API) is read again fresh before it counts**, because a fill may have been indexed after the cached read; only the fresh read can lead to a mismatch and a pause. **The first row with a shortfall fails the minute at once**: a table padded with fee rows would otherwise cost a read for every row before the pause, and the pause must come within minutes.
- **Hourly omission check.** Reads the wallets its own feed saw in that hour plus the wallets in that hour's tables. If its feed has an uncovered part of the hour: every registered wallet when there are at most `OMISSION_FULL_MAX` (default 2,000), else an alert saying that hour rests on the daily check. On 999 the daily builder-file check (a complete list of the builder's fills, a day late) stays as it is. Alert only, as today. Everything goes through the same pacer.
  - **Slices.** The wallets are read `OMISSION_SLICE = 10` a tick, because at the paced rate a large hour could take most of an hour and must never hold up verification (the verifier pauses within minutes). The differences are alerted when the last wallet is done, and only then is the hour marked done. Progress is memory only: a restart redoes that hour. A read that fails alerts, waits 10 minutes of chain time and goes on from the wallet that failed (wallets already read are not read again). Wallets in none of the hour's tables are read first: nothing else sees them (a table wallet was read by its row check).
  - **Row checks come first.** A slice costs seconds, and a verifier that is behind must spend them on the rows (it is the check that pauses). After a verified minute the omission check runs only if the next minute is **not yet due**: due is the poster's own rule (`end of the minute + CHECKPOINT_GRACE_SECONDS <= chain time`), read from a fresh chain time because the row reads just took some, and the chain is read again only when a pass could run. Idle ticks (the next file is not there yet) always run it. A verifier replaying hours therefore does no omission reads, and when it catches up one alert says which hours were skipped. A pass that is dropped (a later hour completed first) alerts the differences it had found and how far it got (`read x of y wallets of the first hour`), and prunes the feed as a finished hour would. The skip is always handled before any comparison, since the dropped hour's rows may be gone.
  - **Wait for the feed.** With a feed the check first needs the hour **delivered** (`deliveredPast(end of the hour)`) **or** a gap in it: a trade still on its way, or a coin the next `meta` read finds, would otherwise be missed. It asks `deliveredPast` on each tick and `uncovered` in the same synchronous step right after it, and marks nothing done while it waits. A dead feed shows a gap, so the wait ends; a wait over 2 minutes of chain time is alerted once and goes on. The hour's window is fixed, so, unlike a per-minute window (§5), an outage inside it always shows in `uncovered`.
- **Builder total check, hourly, alert only.** `D = builderRewards − counted fees through the newest verified minute`, skipped while the verifier is more than 2 minutes behind (Hyperliquid's total runs ahead of the tables by about that much, and a change of that lag between two checks is at most 2 of 60 minutes, 3.3% of an hour's fees). The first value is only recorded. After that: `D` grew by more than the tolerance (5 USDC plus 5% of the fees counted since the last check) → "builder fees not in the tables grew by X: a wallet was missed, or a wallet that is not registered pays our builder fee"; `D` fell by more than the tolerance → "the tables count X more than Hyperliquid's builder total: a registered wallet may be paying 0.1% to another builder". State: `{lastD, countedTotal}` in `verifier-state.json`; a missing field reads as a fresh check. A read that fails or has no `builderRewards` is logged and skipped, and tried again after 10 minutes of chain time (not on every tick); a total that stays unreadable for a day is alerted, once a day ("the builder total has not been readable for a day: that check is off until it is").

## 7. The registry

The Worker also writes an index entry, **before** the record: `registry-log/<chainId>/<registeredAt, 10 digits>-<user>.json` (empty body). A failed index write answers 502 and writes no record. A repeat registration still returns at the `exists` check and writes nothing. The record `registry/<chainId>/<user>.json` stays the only truth; the index only says where to look, so a stale or duplicate entry is harmless (read the record; none yet means ignore it this pass).

`R2Registry` keeps every record in memory. The first `list()` and one each hour read the whole `registry/` prefix (a safety net, and how wallets from before this change are found). Every other `list()` lists `registry-log/<chainId>/` with `start-after` set to 10 minutes before the newest `registeredAt` it has seen, but never later than 10 minutes before its clock (Workers' clocks and R2 write delays; one record dated in the future must not move the window past every real key) and reads only unknown wallets' records. `R2.listKeys(prefix, startAfter?)` sends `start-after` on the first page only: the continuation token holds the position after that. Records never change, so they are read 16 at a time (a failed read is tried twice more, 1 s apart, before it fails the pass) and also kept in the process's state dir (`registry-cache.json`), so a restart at 100,000 wallets does not read them all again. `DirRegistry` (anvil) is unchanged. The key literals in `app/src/register/rules.ts` and `ops/src/registry.ts` are pinned to each other by a test that compares what the two functions return, for the record's key and the index's.

A pass that fails part-way (a record read, a list call) counts for nothing but the records it did read, which are kept and saved to the cache file (each is valid, so a failure or a restart does not redo the reads): the hourly timer and the index window move only when a pass completes, so the retry covers what the failed one would have. A full read re-reads a record that was invalid, so `list(true)` can heal one. A full read after the first one that finds a wallet from before the index window logs a warning with its address and `registeredAt` (found late; its fees may be lost); the first pass of a process, which loads everything, is silent. The cache file is a convenience: a corrupt or wrong-version one is ignored with a warning (then the first, full read), and one that cannot be written is a warning, not a failed list.

**The verifier reads in full before it says "not registered".** `Registry.list(full?: boolean)`: `true` makes `R2Registry` read the whole `registry/` prefix now, whatever the hourly timer says (and the hour restarts from it); `DirRegistry` ignores it. With an incremental index the poster's list can be fresher than the verifier's (a record delayed past the 10-minute look-back and found first by the poster's hourly full read), and "is not registered" pauses the contract. So when a parsed file has a row whose wallet is not in the verifier's list, `Verifier.tick` lists again with `list(true)`, once for that tick, before `checkFile` reports it, and uses that fresher list for the rest of the tick. A wallet still unknown after that is the ordinary mismatch. An ordinary tick never asks for a full read, and a table that cannot be parsed fails as before, without one.

## 8. Published files

**`latest.json`** keeps the checkpoint fields, `table`, `vault` and `pops`, and loses `tree` and `users`. It stays one small file a minute. The archive `checkpoints/<M>.json` is unchanged; the verifier reads the same fields as before, and the keeper reads `latest.json` for the newest minute and the archive of the round it settles (below).

**`users/<xx>.json`** (new): `xx` is the wallet address's first byte in lowercase hex, 256 files at most, and only files with wallets in them exist:

```
{ v: 1, chainId, contract, throughMinute, refBlock, root, totalEntitled, sig, publishedAt,
  vault: { amount, pointsTotal },
  users: { "<lowercase address>": { entitlement, fees, mined, fromVault, refined, points, proof: ["0x…"] } } }
```

- Each file carries its checkpoint's signed fields, so it stands alone. `proof` is empty for a wallet with points and nothing earned (not in the tree).
- Stored gzipped with `content-encoding: gzip` and `max-age=60`. Up to about 640 KB raw (230 KB gzipped) per file at 100,000 wallets (measured, §12).
- A minute with a trade changes every proof, so all files change together; a refining share also changes most entitlements. **The round:** written every checkpoint under 2,000 wallets with an entry, every 5th minute under 20,000, every 15th above. A quiet minute (no fee rows, no pop, the root of the last files written) has every 15th minute as its round at any size, so idle wallets cost almost no writes, added 2026-10-31 at the owner's request: such a file proves the same entitlement, and only its vault amount, and so the wallet's share, lags by up to 14 minutes. `latest.json` is still every minute. Round number by `throughMinute % every == 0`. While the poster is behind (the next minute is already due at the tick's chain time) the round is every 15th minute whatever the wallet count, and the only other write is the first since the start, so a catch-up burst spends no time on files nobody reads yet nor on retries; the first checkpoint in step again always writes, so files are never more than 15 minutes old once the poster has caught up.
- Written after the checkpoint's own publish, 8 at a time; one file's failure does not stop the others. They are derived data and not in `pending.json`: a failure alerts (once an hour per text), never holds up checkpoints, and the next checkpoint in step tries again. Also written at the first checkpoint after a start.
- **Every multiple of 15 is a round** at every wallet count and in catch-up too (1, 5 and 15 all divide it). The keeper settles only such minutes (below), so a wallet always has a file with a proof for the newest round the keeper settled, even if the poster never returns.
- **A write is owed when the chain is past the files.** Anyone can settle a published checkpoint (`Checkpoints.settle` is open) and its signed fields are public each minute. In step, off the round, the poster reads `Checkpoints.latestMinute` (with `anySettled`) and writes when the chain's settled minute is newer than the last minute this process wrote files for, so the next file is newer than the chain and Settle & claim works within about a minute, even if someone settles an off-round minute every minute. One chain read a minute off the round, none while behind, and a read that fails is logged and treated as "not past". The last minute written is kept in memory only.
- `buildTree` computes proofs only when asked and stops producing a dump.

**The app** reads `latest.json` as now (signature and chain checks, the Vault card from it) and, for a connected wallet, `users/<xx>.json`:

- A 404 or no entry means zero for that wallet, not an error. Other failures show the existing "files could not be read" text.
- The same checks as `checkLatest` apply to the user file: the signature first, then the minute and the root against the chain. An entry's entitlement is shown, and claimed, only if its proof verifies against the file's root, and a nonzero entitlement with no proof is a mismatch: that wallet's figures (entitlement, points, refining and share) read 0 and the card says so. When the entitlement verifies, the proof proves it; the entry's other numbers (points, refining, the file's `vault`) are display only, shown as the file says, and never sent anywhere. A store could always change them, as it could in the old `latest.json`. A wallet's numbers, its points and its share of the vault all come from the user file, including its own `vault`, so they are consistent with each other.
- What a storage host could do and cannot: it can hide a wallet's entry or serve an older signed file (the wallet sees less, or "Rewards are updating…"), which it could always do by withholding files; it cannot make a false entitlement pass or a claim pay more.
- Claim uses the user file's checkpoint, with the same `claimPlan` rules, and the chain decides which kind:
  - the file is newer than the chain (or nothing is settled): Settle & claim, with the file's own signed fields;
  - the file is at the chain's latest settled minute with the chain's root: Claim;
  - the file is older than the chain's latest settled minute: the app reads `rootOf(file.throughMinute)`, only in that state (an ordinary poll costs no extra call), after the signature check. If it equals the file's root the file still claims (`Mining` pays against any settled minute that is not cancelled), with no "updating" text; a wallet that has already claimed past the file's entitlement gets no plan. If it reads zero (never settled, or cancelled) or another root, the card says "Rewards are updating…" and offers no claim.
- **The keeper settles rounds.** When nobody has settled for `SETTLE_AFTER_MINUTES`, the keeper settles the newest multiple of 15 at or before `latest.json`'s minute (every minute on anvil), with that minute's signed fields from `checkpoints/<round>.json`, only if it is newer than the chain's. Settling an off-round minute would leave wallets with a file for a settled minute that is never rewritten, and above 2,000 wallets, with the poster gone, no proof would exist for any settled minute. A missing or unreadable archive is alerted and tried again on the next pass.
- A connected wallet with no points shows `0.00`, never a dash. Not connected keeps the dash. One exception: while `latest.json`'s `vault.lastPopMinute` is newer than the wallet's file (above 2,000 wallets the file can be 15 minutes old), the file's points are the pre-pop ones and the share could exceed the vault, so the Vault card shows a dash for points and share.

## 9. Later, not now: our own node

A non-validating Hyperliquid node has no per-wallet reads at all: `--write-fills` streams every fill "in the API fills format" to local files (`~/hl/data/node_fills/hourly/`). It needs 16 vCPUs, 128 GB RAM, a 500 GB SSD and Ubuntu 24.04, so it does not run on the Mac and has a monthly cost. Whether those lines include `builderFee` is UNCONFIRMED. `FillSource` (`ops/src/ingest.ts`) stays the seam: a node-backed source could replace the feed and the pacer without touching the checkpoint rules. Decided when real volume asks for it, with the owner.

## 11. Rollout

Testnet first, no redeploy of contracts and no new state directories: the states keep their versions except `ingest.json`, which migrates. Order: restart the poster and verifier with the new code, then deploy the app at once (an app already open needs a reload; until the first user files land a wallet briefly reads zero), then **restart the poster and the verifier once more**: a wallet that registered between the first restart and the app deploy has no index entry (the old Worker writes none), and a restart's first list reads the whole registry, so it is found at once instead of at the next hourly read. `ingest.json` version 2 is one-way: the old code refuses it, so going back means deleting the file. Mainnet starts with these formats and has no such gap. Docs: `docs/OPS.md` (the scale section rewritten, the IP note removed, env, files), `docs/APP.md`, `CLAUDE.md`.

## 12. Risks

- **A fill during a feed gap, by a wallet idle for over 24 hours,** is not counted. Rare and small; the verifier's checks and the daily file report it.
- **Above about 50 distinct active wallets a minute, rewards run late.** Nothing is lost, and §9 is the way out.
- **Two more WebSocket connections per IP** on the testnet Mac, which also runs other bots against a limit of 10. A refused connection is a gap: it reads everyone, as today, and alerts.
- **Checkpoint time, memory and state size at 100,000 wallets** (measured 2026-10-31 with `npm run scale-check -w ops` and `-- 2000`; Apple M4, Node 26.7, default heap limit 4.4 GB; one checkpoint in memory, no I/O). `applyCheckpoint` + `buildTree` take **5.2 s** (the tree 5.0 s, `applyCheckpoint` 0.06 s of a steady minute); with the state copy 5.5 s. The poster and the verifier both do this every minute, so it is a limit on each separately. The time is linear, about 50 µs a wallet for the tree: 25,000 wallets 1.2 s, 50,000 2.5 s, 200,000 10.3 s, 400,000 21.5 s (24.7 s with the copy and `applyCheckpoint`); the time alone stays under 30 s up to about 400,000 wallets (linear extrapolation: 480,000), but **the trade feed's rules bind first**: the checkpoint is computed on the event loop, which also serves the feed's socket, so a block of over about 20 s in a minute makes a gap every minute (`HEARD_MS`), and one of 15 to 30 s can already miss a pong and reconnect, depending on when it falls between pings (measured with the real feed: a 6 s block is safe, a 17 s block just before a ping reconnects, a 22 s block loses 17.4 s, a 31 s block reconnects). At about 62 µs a wallet for the whole blocking stretch, the clean ceiling is about 240,000 wallets. Memory comes next (400,000: 3.1 GB heap, 3.5 GB resident). At 100,000: calculator state 21.9 MB (`poster-state.json` and `pending.json` hold it, and the verifier's state; rewritten each minute, the poster's twice, so both servers need an SSD); heap 720 MB in use, peak resident 1.0 GB (it ran with the heap limit at 640 MB and ran out of memory at 384 MB, so the checkpoint alone needs about 0.64 GB of heap; Node's default heap limit follows the host's RAM, so give each process 4 GB or set `--max-old-space-size`); user files 256, the largest 638 KB raw and 228 KB gzipped, 52.5 MB gzipped per round, 2.5 s of CPU to build, serialize and gzip; `latest.json` 0.6 KB plus the minute's table. At 2,000 wallets: tree 0.10 s, state 0.45 MB, user files 0.9 MB gzipped in all, 194 MB resident.
- **A long feed gap reads every registered wallet.** More than 5 minutes unwatched in a run of gap minutes (a dead feed, a poster down for longer than that), `TRADE_FEED=off` or `--once`: N ÷ 50 minutes a pass at 50 reads a minute, about 33 hours at 100,000 wallets, with no checkpoint published meanwhile for the first minute. The newest finished minute is fixed when a pass starts, so an outage can cost two full passes: up to 2 × N ÷ 50 minutes, about 66 hours at 100,000 wallets, then a burst. Nothing is lost, rewards are late. The fail-safe is deliberate (a dead or changed feed must never hide a trader), and the way out is the own node (§9). Refreshing `through` during a pass to save the second pass was left out: an extreme case, and by then the own node is likely.
- **Above 2,000 wallets, a dead poster plus someone settling an off-round minute** leaves wallets on "Rewards are updating…" until a poster runs again: the user files are written on rounds, anyone can settle any published minute, and with the poster gone no file has a proof for that minute. The keeper settles only multiples of 15, which always have files.
- **A verifier that is always more than 2 minutes behind** runs neither the omission check nor the builder total check, and says so only when it catches up (the omission skip alert; the builder check says nothing). The omission pass reads about 2,000 wallets an hour when idle, about 1,100 at 25 row reads a minute, little or none at 40 and above (computed for 50 reads a minute, about half that at 25, where the idle ceiling is below `OMISSION_FULL_MAX`'s 2,000); a dropped pass alerts what it found and how far it got.
